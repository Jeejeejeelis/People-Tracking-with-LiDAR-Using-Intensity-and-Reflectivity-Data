# Chapter 7: Conclusion

This thesis embarked on a comprehensive investigation into the viability of purely LiDAR-based pedestrian tracking in highly dense, semi-structured environments. By utilizing the 128-beam Pedestrian-Focus Scene Dataset (PFSD), this research rigorously evaluated how different neural network architectures process dense point clouds, and specifically analyzed the mathematical impact of radiometric feature engineering—namely distance-compensated physical reflectivity ($R = I \times d^2$)—on track association and localization.

The findings from this extensive experimental matrix provide definitive answers to the core research questions and establish several critical paradigms for the future of autonomous perception systems.

## 7.1 The Inadequacy of 2D Voxel Compression
A foundational conclusion of this research is the definitive inadequacy of 2D architecture paradigms for high-resolution pedestrian tracking. The PointPillars architecture, acting as a negative control, suffered a complete collapse (achieving 0.0% AMOTA) when exposed to dense 128-beam crowds. By compressing the Z-axis into a Bird's Eye View pseudo-image, the network permanently loses the vertical spatial resolution necessary to differentiate a pedestrian from urban infrastructure (such as traffic poles or signs). This mathematically forces an impossible trade-off between astronomically high false positive rates and catastrophic drops in true recall. The results conclusively validate that True 3D architectures are an absolute necessity for resolving occlusions and spatial ambiguities in dense human crowds.

## 7.2 The Reflectivity Paradox and the Triumph of Feature Decoupling
The central hypothesis—that appending distance-compensated physical reflectivity to spatial coordinates would universally improve track association—was proven false, uncovering a profound "Reflectivity Paradox." 

Forcing Point-Voxel networks to ingest the hardcoded physics equation ($I \times d^2$) systematically ruined tracking accuracy across all 3D architectures. The inverse-square law mathematically amplified distant sensor noise, allowing distant background clutter to mimic pedestrian radiometric signatures. Because standard 3D networks utilize Max-Pooling or Mean-Pooling inside their Voxel Feature Encoders (VFE), these numerical outliers overtook the voxels, resulting in feature collapse and generating high-confidence "ghost" detections that fractured the tracking algorithm.

The definitive solution to this paradox was found in **Feature Decoupling**. By passing raw Intensity ($I$) and raw Euclidean Range ($d$) as completely separate features, the network's AI was forced to dynamically learn the physical calibration via gradient descent. This paradigm shift was overwhelmingly successful. The Decoupled architectures for CenterPoint and VoxelNeXt saw their AMOTA scores skyrocket to ~70.3%, matching the state-of-the-art Hybrid Multi-Scale Fusion Network (HMFN) baseline (71.7% AMOTA). This mathematically proves that for dense Point-Voxel networks, allowing the neural network to implicitly optimize the radiometric-spatial relationship is universally superior to engineered physical equations.

## 7.3 The Multi-Metric Trade-off: Scene Purity vs. Identity Preservation
By bypassing the standard AMOTA evaluation and integrating a custom Multi-Metric Triad (HOTA, IDF1, DetA, AssA), this research uncovered a fundamental tension between detection algorithms and multi-object trackers. 

While Feature Decoupling optimized AMOTA by keeping the scene mathematically "clean" of false positives, the "Kitchen Sink" approach (feeding the network all features: $I, R, d$) proved to be mathematically superior for true **Identity Preservation**. The combination of raw intensity and explicit physical reflectivity created incredibly powerful feature embeddings. Though it generated slightly more background noise (lowering AMOTA), it resulted in the highest HOTA and IDF1 scores in the study, allowing the Kalman filter to perfectly preserve pedestrian identities through severe occlusions. 

Furthermore, this study proved that tracking heuristics (such as cost matrix material penalties) are heavily dependent on the neural network's feature strategy. Applying rigid tracking heuristics to "Smart" (Decoupled) models violently fractured perfect trajectories by double-counting natural sensor flicker as an error. Conversely, these same heuristics served as necessary mathematical crutches to prune false positives out of "Dumb" (hardcoded Reflectivity Swap) models.

## 7.4 Methodological Advancements
Beyond architectural theory, this research proved that state-of-the-art performance can be achieved through rigorous High-Performance Computing (HPC) optimization. By scaling training schedules to 160 epochs, modernizing the PyTorch gradient backends (`torch.scatter_reduce`), and utilizing 4-GPU distributed batch stabilization, our baseline HMFN implementation achieved an **86.35% mAP**, definitively surpassing the original PFSD authors' benchmark of 84.96%. Additionally, we successfully engineered four novel defenses (Z-Score Normalization, Augmentation Resync, Bottleneck Widening, and Radiometric Time Decay) to actively prevent Volumetric Bleeding and Max-Pooling collapse in dense radiometric Voxel grids.

## 7.5 Future Horizons and Dataset Scarcity
The findings of this thesis dictate a necessary paradigm shift for future LiDAR perception research. Because rigid 3D Voxel grids inherently suffer from "Volumetric Bleeding" (where pooling mechanisms flatten localized material edges), future architectures built for 128-beam sensors must pivot toward Hybrid Point-Voxel (e.g., PV-RCNN) or Range-Image networks that preserve continuous surface boundaries losslessly. 

Finally, our cross-dataset generalization attempts highlighted a severe infrastructural bottleneck in the academic community. The domain shift observed when testing our 128-beam trained models on the 64-beam Waymo Open Dataset resulted in a significant drop in pedestrian recall, proving that models are highly sensitive to point cloud density. With the premier high-density crowd dataset (STCrowd) suffering from broken links and inaccessible repositories, there is a critical and urgent need for the publication of new, accessible, ultra-high-density pedestrian datasets. Only with such data can the autonomous driving industry safely validate these advanced tracking paradigms for real-world deployment in complex urban environments.
